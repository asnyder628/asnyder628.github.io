Teaching Philosophy

I believe learning should be something students want to participate in rather than something they simply have to complete. Too often, students view learning as a series of assignments, tests, and grades. While those things are part of education, I want my students to see learning as an opportunity to grow. Whether they are mastering a new reading skill, solving a difficult problem, or exploring a new topic, I want them to recognize their progress, feel proud of what they have accomplished, and develop confidence in their ability to learn.

At the same time, learning cannot be engaging if it is not accessible. As a special education teacher, I work with students who have a wide range of strengths and challenges. Before students can enjoy learning, they need the tools, supports, and confidence necessary to be successful. Some of the most important instructional decisions happen long before a lesson begins. Creating accessible materials, providing appropriate supports, and building strong foundational skills allow students to fully participate in learning and experience success.

One of my greatest professional interests is the design of immersive and game-based learning experiences. Throughout my teaching career, I have explored ways to move beyond traditional lessons by creating virtual museums, interactive learning environments, and cross-curricular experiences that encourage exploration, creativity, and problem-solving. These experiences have reinforced my belief that students learn best when they are exploring, creating, and making discoveries for themselves rather than simply receiving information. I am particularly interested in how immersive learning environments can increase engagement, support accessibility, and provide students with new ways to demonstrate what they know.

My long-term goal is to combine my background in special education with my interests in educational technology, game-based learning, and instructional design. Through my graduate studies in Learning Technologies and Design, I hope to develop the skills needed to create immersive learning environments that transform learning into an experience rather than a task. As I continue to grow as an educator and designer, I want to remain grounded in accessibility and best practices. The most creative idea in the world is not effective if students cannot access it. My goal is to create learning experiences that are both innovative and inclusive, where students can experience the excitement of discovery while still receiving the support they need to be successful. Ultimately, I want students to see learning as an opportunity for growth and leave with the confidence to tackle whatever challenge comes next.
